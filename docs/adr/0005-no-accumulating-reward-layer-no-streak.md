# No accumulating reward layer, no streak

Nothing accumulates across days except the items' own state. The reward layer is exactly four surfaces that exist anyway as domain state — the per-answer feedback, the progress map, the day close, and the one-time terminal event — with no currency, points, XP, badges, collection, or counter spanning days, and **no streak**, decided against the gamification research's own "BUILD, carefully" rating (#4). A streak rewards showing up rather than knowing (engagement-contingent reward, d=−0.40 overall, −0.43 in children — Deci/Koestner/Ryan 1999), needs a second progress surface competing with the map for the headline the map must keep, and is carried at Duolingo by notifications, which an offline account-free app has no business generating. Because domain state keeps genuinely changing for months, this layer is also immune to the novelty decay that inerts badges (d 1.57 → 0.39 → −0.20 by duration).

Hard to reverse as a stance: adding a currency or streak later re-opens every decision justified by its absence — the map as sole headline, the structurally unpunishable unfinished day (nothing records days, so nothing can punish one), and the structural anti-farming property (all reward is attached to the level, which moves at most once per item per day). Surprising because every comparable app in this space has one, including the market leader for exactly this age band. The trade-off, stated honestly: we forgo the goal-gradient effect (Kivetz et al. 2006; Nunes & Drèze 2006, 34 % vs 19 % on identical effort) and the commitment device a recurring target provides — against which #4's own figures put the streak upside at single-digit retention deltas, never measured against learning (issues #9, #8, #4).
